Limitations of the results-to-inputs table

What the table establishes is narrow. Where a row reports a rerun, it is
a reproduction in O8's sense: the producer's implementation, run by
another operator with the expected outputs visible. It shows that
published bytes are what a pinned commit produces. It does not show that
a checker, an expected rule or a reading of the text is right. The
readers written from the text alone (R8, R11) are the exceptions, and
each is one builder's run whose "from the text alone" rests on the
exposure that builder recorded. In R4, two readers were written from the
published rules by their own authors but ran through the producer's glue
with the answers visible, so they come close to an independent record
without being one.

The units differ by row: checks, members, mutants, generated draws
against thresholds, synthetic cases. Counts from different rows should
not be added or turned into a common rate.

As 0148 says, every quantitative claim in the paper must name its
corpus pin. In the table, R8 and R10 are at v0.17.4, R9 compares
v0.17.4 with v0.18.0 and v0.18.1, R11 and R12 are at v0.18.1, and R13
carries its own commit pins.

A comparison and the run it compares are separate evidence. The
92-of-96 figure in R8 is a reviewer's comparison that neither rerun
repeats, and the causes in R11 were written by the session that set
up the build, which had seen the expected outcomes.

Rows that rest on text that v0.1 does not hold can be reported as
proposed, not as conformance to v0.1: the 5.5 fixture records (R2,
R3), the jcs-sha256 members (R12) and W3C-R-033 (R13).

R14 lists only reader verdicts posted on the list. None has been
posted for R3 or R6, so the table says nothing about whether today's
readers can check those two reports.

The run outputs of my own reruns are kept locally; only what the
messages state is on the record, as 0061 and 0064 say for R3 and R2.
