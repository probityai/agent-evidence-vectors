Reader-side limits: what a reader from the text can decide, and where it
answers cannot-tell

Three readers, three evidentiary classes. The runs the paper relies on
involve three kinds of reader, and the limits section should keep their
evidentiary weight apart: (1) a reader written from the v0.1 text alone,
importing none of the reference implementation, with trust starting at
the release cosign.pub; (2) a reader built from the text with the
reference source in view; (3) the reference reader itself. Kind (2)
agreeing with kind (3) is not independence. In one run, a reader written
with the reference source in view returned 235/235 agreement with the
reference reader on the v0.17.4 report corpus, including the W3C-R-016
rejection of member v04b7811e691bd2c9; v0.18.0 later withdrew that
rejection with forty-one others. That 235/235 agreement is therefore
reproduction and, at the same time, evidence against independence: the
reader reproduced the reference's mistake.

What a text-alone reader can decide. On questions the text determines,
an independent reader recomputes from released bytes and reaches the
answer without reference code: cryptographic signature verification
(22/25 receipt signatures verified, with the remaining three failing
exactly as the corpus marks them), semantic verdicts on a suite whose
expected outcomes are fixed (25/25 on the receipt-signature suite), the
signed CORPUS-DIGESTS chain over the listed corpora, and serialization
questions the governing canonical form settles. On integer minus-zero,
the Python reference reader wrote 0 and the Go reader -0 at v0.17.4
(0038); an independent reader from the text returned RFC 8785's answer,
0 (0046), and no verdict depended on that member. Distribution gaps are
visible to such a reader as well: one listed corpus ships in git only and
is absent from the published wheel, so a reader trusting released bytes
and the signed digest list is presented with a manifest it cannot
resolve.

Where the text leaves the answer open. On the forty-three members
rejected under W3C-R-016, a text-alone reader's step reports held both
readings of the section 2 rule (S-15) - moved value as declared versus
moved recomputed - and selected neither, answering cannot-tell (0039;
0098/0101/0102). The reference reader read the declared value (0054);
v0.18.0 moved forty-two of those members to accept (0079) and re-cut the
forty-third, a w3c-f-16 member, with its accept twin (0103). The
section-10 selection changed from 83 reject / 82 accept to 41 reject /
124 accept while the v0.1 text itself did not change. The reader-side
conclusion is twofold. First, cannot-tell is a finding: it locates an
ambiguity in the text rather than silently accepting or silently
rejecting. Second, every quantitative claim in the paper must name its
corpus pin, because the same reader over adjacent corpus releases gives
materially different counts; unpinned reject/accept percentages are not
comparable across releases.

Reproduction hierarchy. The frozen re-runs fall into distinct classes,
and the paper should label each row. Bit-for-bit operator reproduction
of the text-alone reader over all 235 v0.17.4 members (corpus commit
99b47c56...) matched verdicts.tsv (de987220..., 169 lines) and
not-selected.tsv (cd6962f3..., 66 lines) on two independent
environments, Linux arm64 and Linux x86_64 with CPython 3.13.13 and
PYTHONHASHSEED=0. A cross-environment re-run of a pinned implementation
packet at d0cbe773 (CPython 3.13.12, cryptography 46.0.5, fresh
checkout, PYTHONHASHSEED=0) reproduced its 18 tests and 21 selected
outcomes (7 pass / 13 fail / 1 inconclusive) with both file digests
matching; this is category (a) under O8 - same implementation,
independent operator and environment - not an independent
implementation. No rerun that shares the scorer can establish that the
expected verdicts are right, because the expected answers were in view.
Establishing the readings themselves requires readers built without
reference code, and the corpus-mutation history between v0.17.4 and
v0.18.0 is the strongest evidence the group has for why that distinction
matters.
