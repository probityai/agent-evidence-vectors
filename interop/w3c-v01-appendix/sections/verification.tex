\section{Verification}
\label{sec:w3c-verification}

Each v0.1 report in the table can be checked with one command, from an empty directory,
\begin{quote}
\texttt{uvx agent-evidence-vectors==\aevreadrelease{} --read-w3c-report <file>}
\end{quote}
\noindent which prints \texttt{conforms}, or one line per shape error or rejection row, and exits 0 when the report conforms, 1 when it does not, and 2 when the reader reached no verdict. Rows that are not reports (readers' verdicts, comparisons, census counts) are checked by their rerun command instead. The evidence classes are their authors' words: ``reproduction in O8's sense'' (\aevmsg{Oct}{0163}), ``a reader written from the 0087 text alone'' and ``proposed'' (\aevmsg{Oct}{0162}), ``the closest this paper comes to an independent record'' (\aevmsg{Oct}{0150}). The class of every cell this appendix runs is ``automated rerun from the pins, operated by the appendix's author''. Following \aevmsg{Oct}{0163}, units differ by row and no column is summed.

\paragraph{Every report under every reader.} The reader matrix reruns R1, R2, R3, R3-v01, R4 and R5 from their pins, keeps the nine files that declare the v0.1 format, and reads each with eleven readers: the Python reference reader at 0.12.0, 0.13.0, 0.17.4, 0.18.1 and 0.31.0; the Go and Rust readers at v0.18.1 and at the current commit; and the two readers written from the text, Rouiada Abbas's of \aevmsg{Oct}{0039} and Lars Kersten Kroehl's at 6d8ee54b. Those two read corpus members, so each report is wrapped as a member with an adapter that writes \texttt{\{"id": <input>, "subject": <report>\}} and nothing else. Every report conforms under every reader except R4's three, which the Python readers of 0.12.0 to 0.17.4 reject under W3C-R-020. No cell contradicts a verdict posted on the list (R14).

\paragraph{Negative controls for the readers.} A reader that accepted everything would pass R14, so each reader also reads single-field copies of the reports, each aimed at one rejection row: 153 copies per reader, plus 7 positive controls whose check identity is non-ASCII and whose root is recomputed over the RFC 8785 form. The Python reader from 0.18.1 on and the Go and Rust readers reject 153 of 153 and accept the 7 positive controls. The Python readers of 0.12.0 to 0.17.4 reject 146; they accept the 7 copies whose root is computed over the ASCII-escaped form and reject the 7 positive controls, the cause Kenne Ives gave for R4 (\aevmsg{Oct}{0150}). A declared moved that disagrees with the recomputed value is aimed at W3C-R-021; a recomputed moved outside compared is W3C-R-016.

The readers written from the text answer cannot-tell where the text leaves the reading open, as the reader of 0039 was built to (\aevmsg{Oct}{0039}), so their results are split by what the copy is aimed at. These are findings for their authors, not faults, and go to them first.
