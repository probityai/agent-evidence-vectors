      An Audit Record Format for AI Agent Authorization Decisions
                draft-gilda-audit-agent-audit-record-04

Abstract

   This document defines a record format for AI agent authorization
   decisions and the effects that followed them.  The format is one in-
   toto predicate type, signed inside a DSSE envelope.  A record carries
   the decision and the observed effect with a derived agreement between
   them, whether the decision point could evaluate the request and how
   fresh each input it relied on was, and a canonicalization contract
   under which a party that trusts neither the agent nor its operator
   recomputes the record's consistency from its bytes.  The document
   also defines what a party that retains a record keeps beside it, so
   that the record can later be dated from anchors that party does not
   control.  The format carries the seven minimum audit fields of the
   WIMSE AI Identity Management System framework, and it is offered as a
   data model for the audit records the proposed AUDIT working group
   would specify.  It defines no policy.

1.  Introduction

   This document defines one record format for an agent's authorization
   decisions and their effects, and it defines nothing else.  It
   specifies no policy model, no compliance criteria and no retention
   period, and it does not extend or amend [AIMS].

   Two bodies of text ask for such a format.  Section 11 of [AIMS]
   requires audit records for agent authorization decisions and leaves
   their form open.  The proposed charter of the AUDIT working group
   [AUDIT-CHARTER] asks for data models that represent "interaction
   records, references to the identities of participating entities,
   delegation context, authorization state over time, or action
   provenance", and states that "audit information must be verifiable by
   a party that trusts neither the agent nor its operator".  Appendix C

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   maps each problem the AUDIT BoF request [AUDIT-BOFREQ] and that
   charter name to the section of this document that addresses it, and
   names the ones this document leaves to other work.

   This document replaces draft-gilda-wimse-agent-audit-record-02.
   Sections 1 to 8 and Appendices A and B keep the numbers they had
   there, so a reference to one of those sections of that revision
   resolves to the same section here.

   The goals are:

   *  to make an audit record from one deployment comparable with one
      from another;

   *  to make "tamper-evident" a property a verifier can test on the
      bytes it holds;

   *  to carry the authorization decision and the observed effect in one
      signed record, so a disagreement between them is detectable;

   *  to give the seven minimum fields of Section 11 of [AIMS] one
      member each.

   Two implementations that each satisfy Section 11 in their own format
   produce audit trails that cannot be compared, correlated across a
   service boundary, or checked by a party that trusts neither producer.
   Section 11 asks for all three.

   Comparable has a narrow meaning here.  Two records are comparable
   when a third party holding both can decide, from the bytes alone,
   whether they describe the same request, whether their decisions
   agree, and whether either was enforced.

   Two deployments that each hash a request differently cannot join
   their records on a request digest, so a delegation that crossed
   between them is reconstructable in neither log.

   Section 11 of [AIMS] is normative about the existence of agent audit
   records and silent about their form.  It requires that "deployments
   MUST produce durable audit logs covering authorization decisions and
   subsequent remediations", that "Audit records MUST be tamper-evident
   and retained according to the security policy of the deployment", and
   that audit events record seven minimum fields.

   Section 12 then places the policy model and document format out of
   scope, stating that they are "not recommended as a target for
   standardization within this specification".  Section 13 places
   compliance criteria out of scope, and Section 16 records no IANA

3.  Terminology

   The following terms are used throughout this document. _Observer_,
   _decision point_, _interval_, _retaining party_, _anchor_, _skew
   tolerance_ and _tier_ are defined here; the rest are taken from the
   documents named beside them.

   Agent, Tool, Service, Resource, LLM:  as [AIMS] uses them.

   Agent identifier:  a WIMSE identifier as defined in [WIMSE-ID].

   Statement, subject, predicate:  as [IN-TOTO] uses them.

   Observer:  the party that produces a record under this document.  An
      observer watches from a layer the agent cannot address.  A host-
      side view of a guest filesystem, a hypervisor-level read of guest
      state, and kernel-level supervision below the observed process are
      such layers.  An in-process library, a wrapper the agent links,
      and an importer holding another party's log are not.

   Decision point:  the party that evaluated the authorization request,
      whatever its architecture.

   Interval:  the period between the before-state and the after-state
      that one record covers.

   Retaining party:  a party that keeps a record as evidence, whether it
      produced the record or received it from another party.

   Anchor:  evidence, obtained from a source the retaining party does
      not control, that a given digest existed no later than a given
      time: an [RFC3161] time-stamp token, an inclusion proof in an
      append-only log [RFC9162], or an OpenTimestamps proof.



Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                  [Page 5]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   Skew tolerance:  300 seconds.  Every comparison in this document
      between times written by two different parties allows it, except a
      comparison with the time of an opentimestamps anchor, which allows
      the larger tolerance Section 4.7.2 gives for that kind.  A relying
      party MAY apply a smaller tolerance and MUST NOT apply a larger
      one.

   Tier:  the degree to which a record's own members corroborate what it
      claims about how it was observed.  A verifier derives the tier
      under the rule in Section 5.7 and never reads it from the record.

   Every digest in this document is written as lowercase hexadecimal in
   the algorithm the record declares in its hashAlgorithm member, except
   where the member that carries the digest states its own algorithm or
   encoding.

4.  Audit record specification

   A record is an in-toto Statement [IN-TOTO] carried in a DSSE envelope
   [DSSE].  Its predicateType is

   https://probityai.github.io/agent-evidence-vectors/predicate/v1/\
   agent-audit-record

   Seventeen required predicate members and four optional members are
   defined in Section 5.  Each of the seventeen MUST be present unless a
   rule in that section makes it conditional, no member has a default,
   and a verifier MUST NOT supply one for an absent member (vectors F1
   and EV3).  Two members are conditional on a sibling value,
   resource.argumentsDigest and correlation.externalAnchor, and each is
   specified where it is defined.  The optional members are principal
   (Section 5.2.1), whose absence says nothing about how the request was
   authenticated beyond agent.authentication; dataSubject
   (Section 5.2.2), whose absence says nothing about whose data the call
   read; oversight (Section 5.5), whose absence says nothing about
   whether a person acted; and sequence (Section 5.10), whose absence
   says nothing about how many records the producer issued.

   A verifier MUST reject a value outside any closed vocabulary this
   document defines, and MUST NOT ignore the member (vector V1).  The
   closed vocabularies reuse terms registered in [VOCABULARY].  This
   document cites a registered term by its term URI, https://w3id.org/
   agent-evidence/vocab/v1/ followed by the term key, and a registered
   value by its value URI, the term URI with the value key as its
   fragment.  The registry is release 0.8.0.  Its registryDigest, the
   SHA-256 of its exact bytes, is

4.1.  Canonical form

   Two implementations derive the same bytes from the same Statement
   only if they agree on the serialization and on what makes a Statement
   malformed.  Both are fixed below.

4.1.1.  JSON profile

   Producers and verifiers MUST canonicalize the Statement with
   [RFC8785], and the signature covers those bytes and no other
   serialization (vector T2).  Producers and verifiers MUST enforce the
   [RFC7493] I-JSON safe-integer profile: an integer of magnitude at or
   above 2^53 makes the Statement malformed (vector T5).

   Every number in a Statement MUST be an integer, and MUST be written
   as one: an optional minus sign followed by digits, with no fraction
   part and no exponent.  A number written with a fraction part or an
   exponent makes the Statement malformed whatever its value, so 0.5,
   1.0 and 1e2 are each refused (vector X1).  A verifier MUST NOT round,
   truncate or convert such a number, and MUST NOT accept it as the
   integer it equals.  [RFC8785] serializes a number by the ECMAScript
   algorithm, which writes 1.0 as 1 and 1e2 as 100, so a verifier that
   accepted those tokens would derive the same canonical bytes from two
   different documents, and a signature made over one would verify over
   the other.  No member this document defines takes a number that is
   not an integer, so the rule costs a producer nothing.

4.1.2.  Duplicate members and nesting depth

   A member name repeated at any depth makes the Statement malformed.  A
   verifier MUST reject such a Statement and MUST NOT retain any one
   occurrence (vector T3).  A Statement nested deeper than 128 levels
   MUST be rejected (vector T4).

4.2.  Subject convention

   A record carries two subject entries.  The first is the request, by
   digest: its name is the correlation identifier and its sha256 digest
   is the request digest defined in Section 5.  The second is the after-
   state root of the interval the predicate carries, and its name is the
   first entry's name with the suffix /after.

   "subject": [
     { "name": "urn:example:corr:7f3a",
       "digest": { "sha256": "9f86d081884c7d659a2feaa0c55ad015..." } },
     { "name": "urn:example:corr:7f3a/after",
       "digest": { "sha256": "60303ae22b998861bce3b28f33eec1be..." } }
   ]

   A verifier MUST bind on the digest map and MUST NOT bind on either
   name.  A name is a correlation identifier a producer chooses; a
   digest is a value a verifier recomputes from the bytes it holds.  The
   names exist so that a reader can tell the two entries apart.

   The second entry MUST be present if and only if the predicate carries
   an interval (vectors S1 and I1r), and its digest MUST equal the
   interval's after-state root (vector S2).

   A decision that was denied, and a permit under which nothing was
   written, still carry an observed interval.  The condition on the
   interval is what was observed in it, not what was decided: an
   interval with no writes has equal before-state and after-state roots
   (vectors A2 and E6), and a write the observer saw in the interval is
   retained whatever the decision.  A write attributed to a denied
   request yields agreement of disagree under Section 5.3 (vector A3),
   and a write attributed to another request moves the roots and leaves
   this record's agreement as it is (vector A7).  A consumer therefore
   reads an absence of effect from the members of a record it holds, and
   never from a record it does not have.

4.3.  Timestamps and correlation

   Every time value in a record MUST be an [RFC3339] date-time.  A
   producer SHOULD express it in UTC with the "Z" offset, so that two
   records from different deployments sort together without a conversion
   step.  The correlation identifier is the first subject entry's name,
   so one identifier binds the request digest, the interval and the
   record.

4.4.  Tamper-evidence

   A record satisfies the tamper-evidence requirement of Section 11 of
   [AIMS] when all three of the following hold.

   The DSSE pre-authentication encoding covers the [RFC8785] canonical
   bytes of the whole Statement, and every one of the seven minimum
   fields is inside that pre-image.

   DSSE PAE = "DSSEv1" SP len("application/vnd.in-toto+json") SP
              "application/vnd.in-toto+json" SP
              len(JCS(statement)) SP JCS(statement)

   The request digest, the ordered write chain of the effect, and both
   agreement values are recomputable from other members of the same
   record.  A verifier therefore still refuses a record that the key
   holder altered inconsistently and signed again (vector T1).

   These recomputations detect internally inconsistent alterations even
   when the altered record is signed again by the key holder.  They do
   not detect a coherent replacement in which the dependent members are
   changed consistently and signed again.  A key holder that keeps
   authorityDigest, beforeRoot, recordId and witnessNonce, so that the
   commitment digest still recomputes, and replaces the writes,
   afterRoot, the second subject entry and every value derived from
   them, produces a record that every recompute accepts.  A coherent
   replacement is found only by comparison with something the key holder
   does not control, such as a copy of the original envelope or an
   anchor over its digest that another party obtained (Section 4.7).

   The canonical form is fixed as in Section 4.1, so two verifiers
   derive identical bytes from identical documents.  Any one of the
   three failing is a refusal.

   The retention half of the Section 11 requirement is a property of a
   deployment, and a record cannot assert it about itself.  A record
   under this document SHOULD state that in its doesNotAssert member, so
   that a reader need not infer coverage.  Section 4.7 defines what a
   retaining party keeps beside a record so that the record can still be
   dated and validated after its keys and algorithms age; how long a
   deployment keeps it remains the deployment's policy.

   Each field also carries an evidence partition in fieldEvidence,
   stating whether the observing substrate covered the value or the
   producer asserted it.  All seven fields can be recorded by an agent
   about itself.  A record that says which ones were is checkable; a
   record that does not say is not.

   The fourth field pairs an action with a decision, and this format
   carries decision, effect and the derived agreement together.  A
   record whose decision and effect disagree is well formed, and the
   disagreement is visible in signed bytes:

   "decision": { "action": "write", "reported": "deny" },
   "effect":   {
     "observed": "occurred",
     "writes": [ { "path": "/srv/ledger/settlements.jsonl" } ]
   },
   "agreement": "disagree"

   A log that carries the decision alone records that case as a clean
   denial, because it has no other value to disagree with.

4.6.  Verifying a record

   A verifier holds a DSSE envelope and reaches a verdict from the bytes
   inside it.  The checks below are the ones this document has already
   stated, in the order a verifier can apply them without reading a
   member twice.

   The JSON profile comes first, applied while the payload is parsed:
   the safe-integer bound of [RFC7493], no member name repeated at any
   depth, no nesting past 128 levels, and no number written with a
   fraction part or an exponent.  Each of the four makes a Statement
   malformed, so a verifier that finds one of them has nothing further
   to check.  It has to come first because the signature covers
   [RFC8785] bytes, and a verifier derives those bytes from the parsed
   Statement: a parser that keeps one occurrence of a repeated name has
   already done what Section 4.1 forbids before any signature is checked
   (vector T3).

   The envelope's signature comes second, over the [RFC8785] canonical
   bytes of the Statement that parse produced.  A statement whose
   signature does not verify over those bytes is malformed, and nothing
   further is read from it.  That includes a statement signed over the
   bytes it carries, which is what a DSSE verifier that does not derive
   the canonical form checks (vector T2).






Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 11]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   Membership comes third.  It starts with the Statement's two type
   members (Section 4.8), and then every one of the seventeen required
   members is present unless a rule in Section 5 makes it conditional on
   a sibling value, and every value sits inside the closed vocabulary
   its member declares.

   The recomputes come next, and a signature cannot supply them.  The
   three recomputes that Section 4.4 requires come first: a verifier
   derives the request digest from the four members Section 5 names and
   compares it with the first subject entry, it replays the write chain
   from the before-state root and compares the result with the second
   subject entry, and it derives both agreement values from the members
   they are functions of.

   A fourth recompute binds the prior commitment.  It does not bind the
   record's content.  A verifier recomputes the commitment digest,
   checks that the commitment precedes the interval, and checks that the
   commitment key is absent from agent.signers.  A fifth derives, from
   evaluation.inputs, the set of inputs that were stale or never
   established at decision.reportedAt, and compares it with
   evaluation.status and unavailableInput (Section 5.4).

   A record that carries principal is then checked under Section 5.2.1,

   each anchor over the envelope digest under the offline rule for its
   kind, takes the time of the earliest anchor that validates as the
   time the record is established to have existed, and, for a record
   received from another party, checks the stated receipt time against
   the bracket Section 4.7 defines.  The dating check produces its own

   simply not dated.

4.7.1.  The dating check

   A verifier validates each anchor under Section 4.7.2.  The
   established time of the record is the time of the earliest anchor, of
   either origin, that validates over envelopeDigest.  An anchor that
   does not validate dates nothing and makes nothing malformed (vector
   R8).  No issuance time a record states establishes when it existed.

   A retaining party that sets received to true MUST obtain an anchor of
   origin retainer on receipt, within the anchorWithin it declares, and
   an entry with received of true and no such anchor is malformed
   (vector R4).  The retention time is the time of the earliest anchor
   of origin retainer that validates.  A verifier MUST reject the entry
   as malformed when any of these holds:

   *  receivedAt is earlier than the record's issuedAt by more than the
      skew tolerance (vector R6);

   *  receivedAt is later than the retention time by more than the skew
      tolerance (vector R3);

   *  the retention time is later than receivedAt, or than issuedAt for
      a record the retaining party produced, by more than anchorWithin
      plus the skew tolerance (vector R7).

   A malformed entry does not change the envelope's verdict.  A relying
   party MUST NOT apply an allowance it grants to received records, such
   as dating a record from its receipt, on a malformed entry or on an
   entry in which no anchor of origin retainer validates.

   This check is a consistency check, not proof of receipt.  Its lower
   edge, issuedAt, is a value the sender signed, and the signature binds
   the value without saying anything about the sender's clock.  Its
   upper edge is an anchor over a digest, which shows only that the
   retaining party held the digest by that time, and a retaining party
   can hold the digest before the envelope arrives.  A digest anchored
   before delivery therefore does not establish when the retaining party
   received the record.  A verifier MUST report a receivedAt that passes
   the check as asserted and consistent with the anchors, and MUST NOT
   report it as established (vector R5).  The check rests on two



Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 15]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   assumptions, stated so that a relying party can decide whether it
   holds them: that the sender's clock was correct when it signed, and
   that the retaining party anchored on delivery and not before.

   A retaining party also chooses when it anchors.  One that anchors
   late can state a correspondingly late receipt, and nothing in the
   entry contradicts it, because the bracket's upper edge is an anchor
   whose time the retaining party chose.  The check removes the cheaper
   case, a receipt time stated with no anchor at all, and what remains
   is an assumption that the retaining party anchored promptly after the
   true receipt, within the anchorWithin it declared.  That assumption
   is narrower than trusting the retaining party's statement of receipt,
   and no function of the entry tests it.  An opentimestamps anchor
   widens the room it leaves: its time can lie up to 7200 seconds after
   the moment its block existed, so a retaining party whose upper edge
   is such an anchor can state a receipt up to that much later than a
   prompt anchor of another kind would allow.

4.7.2.  Anchor validation rules

   Each kind of anchor rests on one of two bases.  A hash commitment is
   validated by recomputation to a value the verifier holds
   independently, and a signed token is validated by a signature under a
   path the verifier trusts.

   A kind names the proof format and not only its basis.  A verifier
   validates an anchor offline from the proof alone, and both the
   recomputation or signature check and the source of the anchor's time
   differ by format; a value that named only the basis would tell a
   verifier neither.  The verifier derives the basis from the kind, as
   stated for each kind below.  A Merkle root that commits to
   envelopeDigest and is published in a Bitcoin transaction is a hash
   commitment of kind opentimestamps when its proof is carried in the
   OpenTimestamps serialization, which expresses the path from
   envelopeDigest through the tree and the transaction to the block
   header as a sequence of commitment operations (vector R16).  A hash
   commitment published in another public ledger has no kind in this
   revision, because the bound on its time depends on that ledger's own
   rules for its timestamps.  A revision that adds a kind for such a
   ledger states that bound, as the opentimestamps rule below states it
   for Bitcoin.

   rfc3161:  a signed token.  It validates when its message imprint
      equals envelopeDigest and its signature verifies under the time-
      stamping authority's certificate, by a certification path in
      validationData from that certificate to a trust anchor the
      verifier holds.  Its time is the token's genTime.




Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 16]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   transparency-log:  an inclusion proof that recomputes from
      envelopeDigest to a tree head.  It is a hash commitment when the
      verifier holds that tree head by a means other than its signature,
      such as a checkpoint the verifier already witnessed, and a signed
      token otherwise: it then validates when the tree head's signature
      verifies under the log's key, by a key or path in validationData
      that the verifier trusts.  Its time is the tree head's timestamp.

   opentimestamps:  a hash commitment.  It validates when the proof
      recomputes from envelopeDigest to the Merkle root in the header of
      a Bitcoin block that the verifier holds independently, as a block
      of the chain with the most work.  Its time is the timestamp in
      that block header.  A miner sets that value and consensus bounds
      it only loosely: it must exceed the median timestamp of the eleven
      blocks before it, and it must be no more than 7200 seconds ahead
      of the clock of a node that validates it.  A header time can
      therefore lie tens of minutes before or after the moment the block
      existed.  Every comparison in Section 4.7.1 that uses the time of
      an opentimestamps anchor allows 7200 seconds in place of the skew
      tolerance (vectors R10 and R11).  The 7200 seconds is a consensus
      bound on one side only.  On the later side a header time can be at
      most 7200 seconds ahead of the clock of a node that validates it.
      On the earlier side consensus bounds the time only by the median
      time past of the eleven preceding blocks, so a header time can lie
      further before the moment the block existed, and 7200 seconds
      there is an allowance this document chooses and not a bound the
      Bitcoin consensus rules supply.  Where both times compared are
      times of opentimestamps anchors, as when a verifier finds the
      earliest anchor, it compares the heights of their blocks and not
      their timestamps.

   An anchor rests only on post-quantum assumptions when its validity
   rests on no quantum-vulnerable algorithm: a hash commitment under a
   hash with an output of at least 256 bits, or a signed token whose
   signature and whole path use post-quantum signature algorithms.  A
   verifier derives this from the proof and the path.  A retaining party
   SHOULD hold at least one anchor that rests only on post-quantum
   assumptions, and SHOULD append a new anchor on a schedule its policy
   sets, before the algorithms protecting its existing anchors weaken,
   using the renewal procedures of [RFC4998] or an equivalent.  Where a
   relying party requires post-quantum anchoring from a transition date,
   as [PQ-AGENT-ID] proposes, this section supplies the anchors, their
   order and the material to validate each one, and the date is the
   relying party's.

4.8.  The Statement and predicate types

   A record's Statement carries two type members.  A verifier checks
   each against a value it holds, and never against anything the record
   says about itself, because a record that named the type it expects
   would set its own check.

   byte for byte (vector X4).  A Statement whose _type is any other
   value is malformed.  That includes another version of the in-toto
   Statement, a Statement type defined by another specification, a
   string that differs from the one above only in case or a trailing
   slash, an absent member, and a value that is not a string.  A
   verifier reads nothing further from such a Statement, because the
   members Section 5 defines are the members of the predicate of a v1
   Statement and are defined for no other Statement type.

   predicateType MUST equal the predicate type URI the verifier was
   configured to accept: the URI of Section 4, or the v2 URI Section 8
   defines for the extended set of Section 5.4.  The comparison is byte

   configuration and not from the record (vector X5).  A Statement whose
   predicateType differs from it is malformed, even where the predicate
   under it meets every other rule of this document.

4.9.  Sets of records

   Two checks in this document need more than one record: the checks
   across records of Section 5.9 and the checks of Section 5.10.  A
   party that runs them is handed the records together, as a set.  This
   section defines the set, so that two verifiers read one set the same
   way.

   A set is one JSON text, read under the JSON profile of Section 4.1.1.
   Its value is an object with one defined member, records, an array.
   Each element of records is one record: a DSSE envelope [DSSE],
   written as the JSON object that specification defines and not as a
   string or as the base64 of one.  The set carries each envelope
   exactly as it was signed.  It adds no signature and asserts nothing
   of its own, so it makes a record no more valid than the envelope it
   carries.

   any record in it, when any of these holds (vector X17):

   *  the text is not JSON;

5.1.  Record identity and hash algorithm

   recordId is the producer's identifier for this record.  It is opaque
   to a verifier except where the commitment digest is recomputed, which
   reads it as one of its four inputs.

   hashAlgorithm names the algorithm every digest in the record is taken
   under.  One value governs the whole record: the subject digests, the
   credential, authority, policy and posture digests, the interval
   roots, the write pre-state and post-state digests, and the commitment
   digest are all taken under it.







Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 21]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   A record carrying digests in two algorithms is therefore not
   representable.  A verifier comparing two roots has to know they were
   taken under the same algorithm, and a per-member choice would let a
   producer choose which comparison a reader could make.

   issuedAt is the time the producer sealed the record, and it MUST be
   at or after the interval's sealedAt because the record cannot be
   issued before the interval it describes has closed.  A verifier MUST
   reject a record whose issuedAt is earlier than
   effect.interval.sealedAt, however far earlier, including a record
   whose issuedAt falls on a day before the interval opened (vectors IS1
   and IS2).  The signature binds the value and does not make it true,
   so the check is on the two stated times and not on any clock.

   A verifier that does not implement the declared algorithm cannot
   recompute anything the record binds.  It has no verdict to reach on
   such a record, and its outcome is a report that it could not read
   one.  That is not a refusal on the record's content.

5.2.2.  The data subject

   A server reached through an agent acting for a member of staff can
   read the account history of a customer.  When the customer later asks
   who accessed their data and why, the record holds the staff member as

   and the purpose of the call, without carrying the customer's
   identifier, so that the server can answer an access request from its
   signed records.

      digest, in lowercase hexadecimal, of the [RFC8785] bytes of the
      object {"salt": S, "subject": I}, where S is the salt in base64url
      and I the data subject's identifier.  A value that is not such a

5.4.  Evaluation

   evaluation says whether the decision point evaluated the request, and
   how fresh each input it relied on was. evaluation.status is one of
   evaluated, not-evaluated (vector EV11).  A decision point records
   evaluated when its reported decision does not depend on any input it
   could not obtain, and not-evaluated otherwise.  Where delegation
   paths are judged independently (Section 4.2 of [EVALUATION]), a
   permit on a fully valid path is evaluated even when an input for
   another path was unavailable, because no value of that input could



Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 28]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   change the decision.  A deny is not-evaluated whenever a path the
   decision point could not judge might have been valid.

   decision.reported always records what the decision point enforced.
   Under evaluated it is the outcome of evaluating the policy.  Under
   not-evaluated it is the outcome the decision point fell back to, and
   it is the member that says whether the decision point failed closed
   (deny) or failed open (permit or permit-with-conditions).  A record
   from a decision point that lost an input and denied therefore carries
   deny, and beside an effect of none derives agree.  Without evaluation
   that record is byte for byte the record of a correctly enforced
   denial (vectors A2 and A10), and an incident review cannot tell the
   two apart. decision.policyDigest names the policy the decision point
   enforced.  Where the policy was the unavailable input, the decision
   point cannot name that policy's digest, so decision.policyDigest is
   the digest of the fallback it applied, as delegation.authorityDigest
   names the deny-all document for a decision taken under no authority
   (vectors A18 and A20).  A decision point that holds the profile a
   request names and finds that it grants no comparison has evaluated
   the request, and records evaluated with deny (vector A2); one that
   does not hold the profile records not-evaluated with policy-source
   (vector A20).

   evaluation MUST carry inputs under both statuses (vector EV13).  It
   is an array with one entry for each input the reported decision
   depended on, each input named at most once (vector FR10).  An entry
   MUST carry each of these three members (vectors FR5 and FR6) and MUST
   NOT carry any other except the consumption member that Section 5.9
   defines (vector FR11):

   *  input, a value from the closed set in the table below (vector
      FR12);

   *  lastResult, the time at which the source produced the last value
      of that input the decision point held, or the literal none if it
      never held one;

   *  maxAge, the maximum age in seconds, a non-negative integer, that
      the decision point applies to that input (vector FR13).

   The reference time for every entry is decision.reportedAt, the time
   the decision point reported its decision.  An input's age is
   decision.reportedAt minus lastResult.  An input is fresh when its age
   is at most maxAge, so an age equal to maxAge is fresh (vector FR2);
   it is stale when its age is greater (vector FR3); and it was never
   established when lastResult is none (vectors A10 and FR4).  A
   lastResult later than decision.reportedAt by more than the skew
   tolerance of 300 seconds is malformed, because a decision cannot rest

   tier is one of voluntary, authoritative.  A verifier MUST recompute
   it and MUST NOT take the declared value as the tier.  Vectors TI1
   through TI5 each declare authoritative while failing one clause, so a
   verifier that reads the declared value passes all five.

   A record is authoritative if and only if all five of these hold, and
   voluntary otherwise: vantage is below-observed (vector TI1);
   priorCommitment is present and complete (vector TI2); pathScope is
   non-empty (vector TI3); coverage.scopeComplete is true, which by the
   rule above leaves no gap inside pathScope (vectors TI4 and TI6); and
   resource.binding is digest-bound (vector TI5).

   A record declaring authoritative that fails a clause is malformed,
   and a verifier MUST NOT downgrade it to voluntary (vector TI1).
   Downgrading would let a producer emit an authoritative-shaped record
   and rely on the verifier to relabel it.

   The opposite mismatch is well formed.  A record declaring voluntary
   while meeting all five clauses is not malformed, and a verifier reads
   it at the tier the recompute gives.  Treating the two directions
   differently is deliberate: a producer that over-claims is relying on
   somebody else to correct it, and a producer that under-claims is not.

   A verifier MUST NOT read a voluntary record as evidence that its
   content corresponds to any independently observed fact (vector A5).

5.9.  Consumption under a cumulative limit

   A decision under a cumulative limit, such as a delegated spending
   limit, depends on the value of consumption-state and not only on its
   freshness.  A permit is correct only when the amount already
   committed under the limit, plus the amount held for earlier permits
   that have not settled, plus this request's amount, is within the
   limit.  An as-of time and a maximum age cannot show that, so an entry
   whose input is consumption-state and whose decision applied a
   cumulative limit MUST carry a fourth member, consumption, and an
   entry for any other input MUST NOT carry it (vector CS6).

   consumption MUST carry each of these members, except that unit is
   absent when asset is present.  It MAY carry supersedes, asset, payee
   and settled, and MUST NOT carry any other member (vector CS9):

   *  limitDigest, the digest of the document that sets the limit: the
      delegated authority delegation.authorityDigest names, or a
      document that authority names;

   *  unit, the unit amounts are counted in; for money, an ISO 4217
      alphabetic code.  A record that carries asset MUST NOT carry unit
      (vector CS37);

   *  limit, consumed, reserved and requested, each a non-negative
      integer in the smallest subunit of unit, or of asset where the
      record carries it (vector CS5): the limit, the amount committed by
      earlier settled calls under it, the amount held for earlier
      permits under it that have not settled, and this request's amount,
      which for a payment that can settle below its authorized amount is
      the authorized maximum;

   *  held, an array with one entry for each unsettled earlier permit,
      whose amounts sum to reserved (vector CS3), and no two of which
      carry the same operationKey (vector CS13).  Each entry carries
      exactly that permit's operationKey, requestDigest and amount, MAY
      carry payee and deadline, and MUST NOT carry any other member
      (vector X6).  Its payee is the payee of that permit's payment,
      written as consumption.payee is written, and its deadline is the
      [RFC3339] date-time until which that permit can still settle;

   *  stateRevision, a non-negative integer naming the revision of the
      consumption state the decision point read.  The entry's lastResult
      is the time that revision was produced;

   *  asset, for an amount counted in an asset that no ISO 4217 code
      names, such as a token on one chain.  It is an object carrying
      exactly id, a [CAIP-19] asset identifier naming the chain and the
      asset (for a token, its contract), and decimals, a non-negative

      6, and USDC on Solana are two assets;

   *  supersedes, for a limit raised or cut while permits are held: the
      digest of the limit document that this record's limitDigest

      limitDigest (vector CS23);

   *  payee, the digest of the [CAIP-10] account identifier of the party

   *  settled, the terms a paid call settled on, defined below.

   A retry is identified by decision.operationKey, a string the agent
   assigns to one intended operation and carries in its signed request,
   so that every attempt at that operation carries the same key.  A
   record whose evaluation.inputs carries consumption MUST carry
   decision.operationKey (vector CS14); a record without consumption
   need not.  Wherever decision.operationKey is present, with or without
   consumption, it MUST be a string of at least one character: a
   verifier MUST reject a record whose decision.operationKey is the
   empty string, or is not a string (vector X7).  The operationKey of
   each held entry is held to the same rule.  An empty key would be one
   key that every caller without a key shares, so two different intended
   operations would read as a retry of each other, and the second would
   not be counted.  The scope of the key is the one given for a claim
   below.  A request whose decision.operationKey appears in held is the
   same operation as the permit held for it, as when a caller retries
   after a lost response: its decision.requestDigest and requested MUST
   equal the held entry's (vectors CS4 and CS10), its payee MUST equal
   the held entry's payee, the two absent together or equal (vectors
   CS41 and CS42), and it is not counted a second time.  A request whose
   key is not held is a new operation and is counted, even when its
   decision.requestDigest equals a held entry's (vectors CS11 and CS12).
   The request digest cannot identify a retry: it is a content digest

5.10.  Sequence

   sequence is optional.  When present it places the record in a
   numbered series its producer issues, so that a party holding several
   records of the series can name one missing from them.  It carries
   exactly two members, and a verifier MUST reject a sequence member
   carrying any other (vector SQ2): boundary, a string the producer
   chooses to name one series, and seq, a positive integer (vector SQ3).
   Two records are in one series when they carry the same boundary and
   their envelopes verify under the same key, and a producer that
   changes its signing key starts a new series.  A producer MUST give
   the first record of a series a seq of 1 and each later record of that
   series a seq one greater than the record before it, and MUST NOT give
   two records of one series the same seq (vector SQ1).

   Within a series, seq is also the running count of records issued, so
   the format carries no separate count member: a count beside a
   contiguous number that starts at 1 would repeat it and add no check.

   Two checks need more than one record, and a party holding records of
   one series runs them.  A number below the highest it holds, which it
   does not hold, names a record that was issued and that it has not
   received: one withheld, or not yet delivered (vector SQ4).  A
   record's identity for this check is the SHA-256 of its decoded DSSE
   payload bytes, the serialized in-toto Statement the signature covers.
   Two records of one series with the same seq and different identities



Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 43]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   are the producer issuing two different records at one place in the
   series, and the set fails the check (vector SQ5); the same record
   delivered twice has one identity and is not a conflict.  Neither
   check reaches a record above the highest number held: a series whose
   last records were withheld reads the same as one whose producer
   stopped issuing, until a later record of it arrives.  A consumer that
   needs that tail bounded SHOULD require a record of the series within
   an interval its policy sets.

   sequence is producer-asserted, and its evidence class is the one
   fieldEvidence declares for correlation.

6.  Security Considerations

   A record is checkable for internal coherence and says nothing about
   the world outside its own bytes.  Where a consumer needs a fact about
   that world, it has to establish that fact somewhere else.

6.1.  The vantage claim is not self-proving

   The vantage claim in a record is an assertion about the world, and
   the record makes it coherent with its own members without making it
   self-proving.  A consumer that requires the vantage to be true MUST
   anchor the observer's key out of band, exactly as it would anchor any
   signer.  Such a consumer MUST read authoritative as coherence with a
   claimed observation.vantage, and never as proof of one (vector A5).

6.2.  A producer that observes only itself

   A producer that observes only itself can satisfy every field of this
   format, and the observation.priorCommitment member raises the cost of
   doing so.  It binds the before-state, the authority and the record
   identifier under the observer's signature before the interval opens.
   A forgery therefore has to be decided on before the forger knows what
   it will be lying about.

   It does not reduce the cost to zero, and a producer holding two keys
   satisfies every clause.  That residual is detectable by a key
   inventory outside the record and by no function of the bytes inside
   it.  A consumer that depends on the vantage SHOULD keep such an
   inventory.










Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 44]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


6.3.  Withheld rows

   A record can omit a remediation event, or a claimed access that the
   observer saw, and no function of a Statement detects a row that was
   never written.  No quantity of extra carried material changes that,
   because extra material is material a withholding producer also
   declines to carry.

   A withheld record and a record never produced are different failures.
   Where records carry sequence (Section 5.10), a record withheld from a
   party that holds a later record of the same series is detectable from
   the records alone, because its number is missing from them.  A record
   never produced leaves no number missing, and neither does a withheld
   record numbered above the highest one held; no function of the
   records detects either.

   The coverage, fieldEvidence and doesNotAssert members exist so that a
   blind spot travels where the observer is honest about it.  An
   observer that knows of a blind spot SHOULD record it in the
   doesNotAssert member.  None of these members is a defence against a
   producer that conceals one.

6.4.  Well-formed disagreement

   A disagreement between the reported decision and the observed effect
   is well formed, as is a disagreement between the two postures, and as
   is a remediation event recording that enforcement did not occur.  A
   verifier MUST accept all three (vectors A3 and A4).

6.5.  A decision that was not evaluated

   A record whose evaluation.status is not-evaluated is well formed
   beside any reported decision and any agreement, and a verifier MUST
   accept it (vectors A10, A12 to A14, and A18 to A20).  A deny that was
   not evaluated is a decision point failing closed, and a permit that
   was not evaluated is one failing open.  Both are facts an audit trail
   exists to carry.  A consumer SHOULD treat a not-evaluated permit as
   an enforcement failure for policy purposes, and this document leaves
   that to the consumer.

   The reason is the one Section 6.4 gives for a disagreement.  A format
   with no way to say that a decision point could not evaluate records
   every outage as policy: each lost standing source reads as a
   correctly enforced denial, and each fail-open reads as a permit
   somebody decided.  An incident review that reads such a trail finds
   no outage, because none was ever written down.





Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 45]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   Every source a decision point reads is also a way to stop it
   (Section 5 of [EVALUATION]).  An attacker who can make a source
   unavailable turns fail-closed evaluation into a denial of service,
   and the not-evaluated records are the evidence of it.  A consumer
   SHOULD read a rate of not-evaluated records naming one input as an
   availability event, not as a series of policy decisions.

   evaluation is producer-asserted.  A decision point that records
   evaluated for a decision that depended on an input it did not obtain
   is withholding, as in Section 6.3, and no function of the record's
   bytes detects it.  The same holds for evaluation.inputs: the
   recompute catches an entry whose own times contradict the status, and
   it cannot catch a lastResult written later than the source produced
   the value, or a hop that wrote the time it received a forwarded value
   in place of the source's.  A consumer that relies on freshness SHOULD
   read each entry's evidence class as the one fieldEvidence declares
   for decision.

6.6.  Revocation without a bound

   Section 11 of [AIMS] requires that revoked or downgraded
   authorization be enforced "without undue delay" and specifies no
   bound.  This document carries signalReceivedAt and enforcedAt, and
   defines no bound of its own.  A record with an arbitrarily large gap
   between them is well formed.  A consumer SHOULD set a bound on that
   gap as policy and read a record that exceeds it accordingly.

6.7.  Relationship to other tamper-evidence mechanisms

   A signature over canonical bytes detects an editor who cannot sign.
   A trusted timestamp [RFC3161] adds an assertion about when the bytes
   existed, and an append-only log [RFC6962] [RFC9162] adds an assertion
   that they were published.  This document requires neither, and a
   deployment MAY carry a record under this format in such a log.

   None of the three detects a producer that signed a false record.  The
   recomputes in Section 4.4 refuse a record whose members contradict
   one another, whoever signed it.  These recomputations check
   consistency among the represented fields and do not establish the
   truth of external events.

6.8.  Two implementations that both differ from the canonical form

   Appendix A gives the mechanism by which two implementations can share
   a serialization that is not [RFC8785].  Neither party can see the
   failure from inside it.  Every signature each of them produces
   verifies for the other, and the records fail only for a third party
   that canonicalizes correctly.



Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 46]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   A consumer therefore derives the canonical bytes itself and does not
   accept a producer's report of them.  Section 4.1 is normative for
   that reason, and vector T2 is the member that tests it.

6.9.  Receipt time and late anchoring

   A retention entry's receivedAt is asserted by the retaining party,
   and Section 4.7.1 makes it checkable for consistency, not true.  A
   retaining party that anchors a digest before the envelope arrives, or
   anchors late and states a correspondingly late receipt, produces an
   entry that passes the check.  A relying party that grants a received
   record an allowance, such as dating it from its receipt, not its
   issuance, is relying on the two assumptions Section 4.7.1 states, and
   SHOULD limit the allowance to retaining parties it trusts to anchor
   on delivery.

   From the bytes of a retention entry, a source outage, anchoring that
   never happened in time and anchoring that happened late read the
   same. anchorWithin is a statement the retaining party made, and no
   member of the entry can prove that its anchoring source was down, so
   the format does not signal an outage (vector R17).  A relying party
   that needs the commitment to bind requires a second anchor, taken
   after the outage, over the entry that carries anchorWithin.  The
   commitment is fixed before the first anchor exists only if the entry
   that carries it is itself anchored.  The time of an opentimestamps
   anchor is its block header time with the 7200-second allowance of
   Section 4.7.2, and two such times are ordered by block height.

   An anchor of origin received was obtained by the party that sent the
   record.  It can date the record's existence, and it says nothing
   about when the retaining party received it, so it never forms the
   upper edge of the receipt bracket.

6.10.  A retained record is not a live decision

   A retained record is evidence that a decision was made, and a
   verifier that checks it later answers one question: did the producer
   sign this decision, and does the retention entry date it as
   Section 4.7.1 states.  It never answers whether the same action may
   be taken now.  A verifier MUST NOT submit a retained record, its
   decision.operationKey or any nonce, token or credential it carries to
   a live decision point, a nonce or replay ledger, or a consumption
   ledger to check it, and MUST NOT read the present state of any of
   those as an input to the record's verdict.  A nonce that today's
   ledger has already seen says nothing about a record signed before it,
   and a nonce today's ledger has not seen does not make an old decision
   current.  A relying party that wants the action taken again obtains a
   new decision for it, under the authorization state in force at that



Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 47]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   time, and the new decision is a new record.

   The rule runs the other way too.  A decision point MUST NOT accept a
   retained record, or any part of one, as authority for a new action.
   Under Section 5.9, a verifier reads the cumulative limit from the
   records in the set it was given, never from a ledger it queries, so
   the verdict for a set of retained records is the same on every day it
   is computed.

7.  Privacy Considerations

   A record under this document carries an agent identifier, a delegated
   subject, a resource identifier and a set of paths.  The delegated
   subject in particular may identify a natural person.  Deployments
   SHOULD consider which of these a given consumer needs before
   distributing a record.  The doesNotAssert member is the place to
   record what a distributed record deliberately omits.

   The format binds five documents by digest and carries none of them:
   the credential, the authority document, the policy, and both posture
   documents.  A record that carries delegation.rootDigest binds a
   sixth, the delegation root's path or key set, which a retaining party
   keeps beside the record.  A consumer that needs their content SHOULD
   resolve them out of band.

   principal names an issuer's subject, which may identify a natural
   person, and binds the token by digest without carrying it.
   dataSubject names the person whose data a call read only by a keyed
   reference: a holder of the record without the salt cannot recover the
   identifier, and a party that holds the salt can test a guess of it,
   so a server SHOULD keep the salt with the same care as the
   identifiers it protects. consumption.payee and settled.payee are
   digests of account identifiers that are public on their chains, so
   anyone can test a guessed address against them.

   That separation is deliberate and is the privacy-preserving default,
   because it lets a record travel to a party entitled to verify the
   decision without carrying the material the decision was made from.  A
   consumer entitled to the decision receives the digest and resolves
   the document only if it needs the content.

   A retention entry's anchors are over the envelope digest alone, so an
   anchoring service, a transparency log or a time-stamping authority
   learns that a record existed and nothing in it.  A retaining party
   that discloses an entry discloses its receipt time and the parties
   whose validation data it holds, and SHOULD treat the entry with the
   same care as the record.

8.  IANA Considerations

   This document has no IANA actions.  The predicate type is a URI under
   a namespace the author controls and requires no registration to be
   used, consistent with the in-toto Attestation Framework's treatment
   of predicate type identifiers [IN-TOTO].

   A registered identifier would be the alternative.  It is not proposed
   here, because a record carries its predicate type inside the bytes
   its producer signed.  A later revision SHOULD NOT move the URI once
   records carrying it exist, since moving it invalidates every
   signature over them.

   Revision -02 of this document froze the predicate type URI, and its
   Document History says so.  Before it, a revision could change what a
   record under the URI must carry, as draft-gilda-wimse-agent-audit-
   record-02 did (vector EV3) and as draft-gilda-audit-agent-audit-
   record-00 did (vector EV13).  From that revision on, a change to what
   a record must carry, including a value added to a closed vocabulary,
   is published under a new URI, as Section 5 requires, and a record
   valid under the URI stays valid under every later revision that keeps
   it.  The members of release 0.28.0 of [VECTORS] are the records that
   revision froze the URI against.

   This revision adds optional members under the v1 URI: principal and
   dataSubject (Section 5), and supersedes, asset, payee and settled of
   consumption with payee and deadline of a held entry (Section 5.9).  A
   record that carries none of them is judged as under the previous
   revision, apart from the rules the Document History lists as new in
   this revision, so these additions make no record of the previous
   revision invalid.  An earlier revision does not define them, and a
   record that carries one of them is valid only under this revision and
   later ones.

   This revision adds one value to the closed set of Section 5.4,
   principal-standing-source, and so publishes the extended set under a
   second predicate type URI, https://probityai.github.io/agent-
   evidence-vectors/predicate/v2/agent-audit-record, which is the URI of
   Section 4 with v1 read as v2.  A record under the v2 URI follows this
   document with the extended set, and a record under the v1 URI follows
   this document with the set of the previous revision, so a verifier
   never meets under the v1 URI a value it is required to refuse
   (vectors PS1 and PS2).  Every other rule is the same under both.

Appendix A.  Design rationale

   Two requirements of this document are not derivable from Section 11
   of [AIMS] on its own: the canonicalization contract, and carrying the
   observed effect beside the reported decision.  Neither is restated
   normatively here.

A.1.  The canonicalization contract

   The word "tamper-evident" appears once in [AIMS] and is not defined
   there.  Read narrowly it means a record whose alteration is
   detectable, which a signature provides.  Two gaps remain.




Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 55]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   A signature over a serialization is only as good as the agreement on
   which bytes were signed, and that agreement is a property of a
   written contract between two implementations; neither one's code
   establishes it.  Two implementations can agree with each other on a
   canonical form and both differ from [RFC8785], and no amount of
   signing or hash-chaining detects that.

   Each verifies its own records and rejects the other's, or worse,
   accepts a record whose meaning it has silently changed.  A
   canonicalization contract is therefore part of the tamper-evidence
   requirement; it is not an implementation detail, and Section 4.1
   makes it normative.

   A signature also says nothing about the producer.  The party that
   holds the signing key can alter a record and sign it again, and every
   signature check still passes.

   Detecting that requires fields whose values are recomputable from
   other fields in the same record, so that an internally inconsistent
   record is refusable regardless of who signed it.  A hash chain
   catches an alteration by a party with no signing key; only a
   recompute catches the key holder, and Section 4.4 specifies three of
   them.  A recompute catches the key holder only when the alteration
   leaves the record inconsistent with itself.  A coherent replacement,
   signed again, passes every recompute, and Section 4.4 says what finds
   it.

A.2.  The decision and the observed effect

   The fourth of the seven minimum fields pairs "action requested and
   authorization decision".  A record carrying only the decision is a
   self-report: it states what an enforcement point concluded, and a
   deployment that mis-enforces its own conclusion produces a clean
   audit trail of the decision it failed to apply.  Section 11 asks for
   enough to "reconstruct agent behavior and authorization context after
   execution", and behaviour is not reconstructable from a decision.

   This document therefore requires both sides in one record: the
   decision as reported by the enforcement point, and the effect as
   observed.  It also requires an agreement between them, which a
   verifier derives from the two values by the table in Section 5.3; it
   is not a claim the record makes.  A decision reported as denied
   beside an effect observed as having occurred is then a well-formed
   disagreement in signed bytes, which a verifier accepts and a consumer
   can find (Section 6.4).  In a log that carries the decision alone it
   is undetectable.





Gilda                      Expires 23 May 2027                 [Page 56]

Internet-Draft             Agent Audit Record              November 2026


   The same construction applies to the sixth field.  Section 8 of
   [AIMS] defines posture assessment as an evaluation performed at
   credential provisioning, while the sixth field asks for the posture
   "influencing the decision".

   A conformant record may therefore carry a posture that was accurate
   at issuance and inaccurate at decision time, with nothing in the
   record to say which.  Carrying posture as the decision point read it,
   beside the posture as it was observed and its assessedAt, makes that
   difference visible without constraining either value.

A.3.  Why the tier carries five clauses

   Each clause of the tier recompute closes one route by which a record
   could claim an observer's vantage while carrying nothing that
   distinguishes it from a self-report.

   vantage of below-observed is the claim itself, and the four clauses
   under it make the claim cost something.  The prior commitment binds
   the before-state before the interval opens, so a producer cannot
   decide after the fact what it saw.  A non-empty pathScope means the
   record names the ground it covers.  A complete coverage claim, which
   no listed gap inside that ground may contradict, means the record
   does not quietly exclude the interesting part.  A gap outside that
   ground does not count against it, because the record claims nothing
   there.  And a digest-bound resource means the arguments of the call
   are pinned.

   Dropping any one of them admits a record that carries the authority
   of the rest while resting on nothing.  That is why the corpus in
   Appendix B carries one reject member per clause.
