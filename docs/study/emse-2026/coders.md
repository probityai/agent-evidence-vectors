# Label 27 changelog entries by what caused them: an invitation

I'd like you to read 27 changelog entries and label what caused each one. It takes about an hour, you don't need to know the spec, and I need your labels by 8 October 2026.

Some background: I'm writing a paper for the journal Empirical Software Engineering about how a test set for a spec gets better over time. The test set is the one behind a spec I proposed to in-toto (in-toto/attestation#570). Its changelog records 27 revisions, and the paper asks what caused each one. There are six possible causes: an outside implementer, a comparison between my own verifiers, a mutation test, an attack someone found, the test set's own checks, or a change to the spec.

Two labelings of those revisions already exist. Both came from the same AI model, and they disagree about which cause comes first. That model also helped write the test set, so it shouldn't be the one to settle the question. I'd rather people label the entries themselves.

## What the work is

I send you a short codebook that defines the six causes, the 27 entries, and links to the commits behind each one. You label each entry twice. The first time you go by its text alone, and the second time you've read its commits too. Where the text supports more than one cause, give it more than one label.

You fill in a plain table with one row per revision. Nothing needs installing, and you can stop whenever you like. You won't see the existing labels, the other coders' labels or the draft paper until yours are in, so nothing steers you.

## How much time it takes

The first round takes about an hour. The codebook and the 27 entries run to about 24,500 words, and most of your time goes into that first read. The second round takes longer if you read every commit closely.

## Why 8 October

I submit the paper on 14 October. That leaves me a few days to set your labels beside the model's and the other coders'.

## What you get

I'll thank you by name in the paper's acknowledgements, or leave your name out if you'd rather stay anonymous. You also get the full results, including how your labels compared with the model's and with the other coders'.

## How to say yes

Reply where you were invited and say you're in. I'll send you the codebook, the entries, the commit links and the answer sheet in reply.

## Consent

Taking part is voluntary. You can pull out at any time before publication, and if you do, I remove whatever you sent from the study.

I publish your labels and the results from your labels. Your name appears only if you choose: by name in the thanks, under a pseudonym you pick, or not at all.

If you use any tools or AI models, tell me which ones. About how you worked, I record only the tools and models you tell me about and whether you'd seen the other labelings or the draft paper. I publish your session logs only if you agree to that separately. I publish nothing else about you.

To ask a question or pull out, reply in the thread or message where you were invited.

## My competing interest

I wrote the spec and the test set, and I run the study. The paper says so.
