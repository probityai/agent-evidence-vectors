# Label 27 changelog entries by what caused them: an invitation

I'm writing a paper for the journal Empirical Software Engineering about how a test set for a spec gets better over time. The test set is the one behind a spec I proposed to in-toto (in-toto/attestation#570), and its changelog records 27 revisions. The paper asks what caused each one. The six possible causes are:

- an outside implementer;

- a comparison between my own verifiers;

- a mutation test;

- an attack someone found;

- the test set's own checks;

- a change to the spec.

Two labelings of those revisions exist, and both came from one AI model. They disagree about which cause comes first, and a model that helped write the test set should not be the one to settle it. So I'm asking a few people to label the entries themselves.

## What I'm asking

You get a short codebook that defines the six causes, the 27 entries, and links to the commits behind each one. You label each entry twice: once from its text alone, then again after reading its commits. More than one label per entry is fine where the text supports it.

No knowledge of the spec is needed. The answer sheet is a plain table with one row per revision, nothing needs installing, and you can stop whenever you like. You won't see the existing labels, the other coders' labels or the draft paper until yours are in.

## How long it takes

About three hours. The codebook and the 27 entries run to about 24,500 words, around two hours of reading for the first labels. The second labels take longer if you read every commit closely.

## By when, and why

Please send your labels by 8 October 2026. I submit the paper on 14 October, and I need the days between to compare the labelings.

## What you get

Your name in the paper's acknowledgements, or no name if you'd rather stay anonymous. You also get the full results, including how your labels compared with the model's and with the other coders'.

## How to say yes

Reply where you were invited and say you're in. I'll send the codebook, the entries, the commit links and the answer sheet in reply.

## Consent

- Taking part is voluntary. You can pull out at any time before publication, and whatever you sent is then removed from the study.

- What gets published: your labels and the results worked out from them.

- Your name appears only if you choose: by name in the thanks, under a pseudonym you pick, or not at all.

- What I record about how you worked: the tools and models you tell me you used. I also record whether you had seen the other labelings or the draft paper.

- Session logs are published only if you agree to that separately. Nothing else about you is published.

- To ask a question or pull out, reply in the thread or message where you were invited.

## Competing interest

I wrote the spec and the test set, and I run the study. The paper says so.
