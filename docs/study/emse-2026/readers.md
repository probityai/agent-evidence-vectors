# Read some test statements against a spec: an invitation

I'd like you to read some test statements against a spec and tell me, from the spec alone, what the right answer to each one is. Ten statements take about an hour, and I need your readings by 8 October 2026.

I'm asking because I'm writing a paper for the journal Empirical Software Engineering about a test set I built for a spec I proposed to in-toto, the Adversarial Execution Evidence predicate (in-toto/attestation#570). I wrote every expected answer in that test set myself, and nobody outside my group has checked them. A test set is only as good as its answers, so I want careful readers who don't share my assumptions.

## What the work is

I send you the spec and a batch of test statements under plain labels, with my answers hidden. For each statement, you decide whether it's valid, invalid, or not decided by the spec. If it's valid, you write down the result the spec says it produces. Then you quote the sentence of the spec that settles it.

"The spec doesn't say" is a real answer, and I count it as one. You fill in a plain table. Nothing needs installing, and you can stop whenever you like.

## How much time it takes

There are 272 statements, and I'm aiming for two readings of each, so any number you can take from ten up helps.

Most of your time goes into the first read of the spec, whose rules run to about 18,500 words. Once you've read it, each statement goes quickly, so ten statements take about an hour.

## Why 8 October

I submit the paper on 14 October. I need the days in between to settle any disagreements.

## What happens when you disagree with me

That's exactly what I'm hoping to find. Three people outside my group settle each disagreement. If fewer than three are available, I report it as unsettled. I get no vote. I publish every disagreement, along with how it was settled.

## What you get

I'll thank you by name in the paper's acknowledgements, or leave your name out if you'd rather stay anonymous. You also get the full results, including how your readings compared with mine and with the other readers'.

## How to say yes

Reply where you were invited and tell me how many statements you'd like. I'll send you the statements and the answer sheet in reply.

## Consent

Taking part is voluntary. You can pull out at any time before publication, and if you do, I remove whatever you sent from the study.

I publish your readings and the results from your readings. Your name appears only if you choose: by name in the thanks, under a pseudonym you pick, or not at all.

If you use any tools or AI models, tell me which ones. About how you worked, I record only the tools and models you tell me about and whether you'd seen the test set, its changelog or other implementations. I publish your session logs only if you agree to that separately. I publish nothing else about you.

To ask a question or pull out, reply in the thread or message where you were invited.

## My competing interest

I wrote the spec and the test set, and I run the study. The paper says so.
