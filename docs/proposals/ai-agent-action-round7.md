# AI Agent Action predicate: four items for the next review of in-toto/attestation#588

The conformance corpus under `vectors-ai-agent-action/` in this repository is now regenerated against #588 at `a5dd509`. Its vendored copy of the specification recomputes to `specDigest` `5c417e9cb61fa61daaaa7ecf18a5c50f83bb4160635dc353920d3f9d3de73d4c`, which is the SHA-256 of the specification's own bytes at that commit, so the condition the conformance-corpus subsection sets at lines 660-664 for certifying this revision is met by the next tagged release. The three rules the previous review round added now each have vectors that exercise them from both sides: a segment rooted at a `chain_break` with `priorHead: null` is identified by the break record's own chain hash (lines 781-794), a break whose prior head is known is not a root (lines 766-773), and a deployment claiming resistance against a compromised attestor forbids `priorHead: null` (lines 1271-1274). Building those vectors turned up the four items below. Each quotes the text at `a5dd509` by line and proposes replacement wording.

## 1. Name the carrier of the deployment profile

Lines 1271-1274 make a MUST conditional on the deployment: "A conformant deployment claiming resistance against a compromised attestor MUST forbid `priorHead: null` in the deployment profile. Producers configured this way MUST NOT emit a `chain_break` with `priorHead: null`, and verifiers MUST reject, fail-closed, any such record they receive." The text does not define "deployment profile" and does not say where the claim is carried. A verifier holding a Statement and its log has no way to tell, from those bytes alone, whether a `priorHead: null` break it receives is to be rejected or accepted and surfaced as the unattested discontinuity lines 1275-1277 describe. Two conforming verifiers can therefore reach opposite verdicts on the same record, and a conformance corpus cannot say which verdict it expects without naming the claim itself. The corpus now does that: each member whose verdict depends on the claim names the profile it holds under, and a rail says which profiles it runs.

Proposed text, to follow line 1277: "The deployment profile is an input to the verifier, not a member of any record or Statement: it states whether the deployment claims resistance against a compromised attestor. A verifier MUST accept the profile as configuration, MUST apply the prohibition above when the profile makes that claim, and MUST state in any conformance or verification report which profile it applied. A record or Statement carries no member that selects the profile, because the attestor whose compromise the claim defends against would control that member."

The last sentence is the reason not to put the claim in `metadata` or in a Statement annotation: a restarted, compromised attestor would simply write the permissive value.

## 2. Walk from the root record, not from the genesis record

Lines 700-704 require the verifier to "reconstruct the chain as a strict walk from the genesis record rather than by checking that each record's `previousHash` appears somewhere in the presented set." Since the previous round, a chain has two kinds of root (lines 771-773: "a root is either a genesis record or a `chain_break` with `priorHead: null`"), and a break-rooted segment has no genesis record at all. Read literally, lines 702-703 give a verifier no starting point for such a segment, so one implementer rejects every break-rooted segment for lacking a genesis record while another walks it from the break. The same assumption survives at lines 708-709, "Since the subject digest is the genesis hash, both branches carry the same subject digest", which is now true only of genesis-rooted chains.

Proposed text for lines 702-703: "MUST reconstruct the chain as a strict walk from its root record, which is the genesis record or a `chain_break` with `priorHead: null` (see Genesis and breaks), rather than by checking that each record's `previousHash` appears somewhere in the presented set." Proposed text for lines 708-709: "Since the subject digest is the root record's chain hash, both branches carry the same subject digest".

## 3. Make line 748 reject every record that lacks its linkage, and settle the null-break case once

Lines 746-747 say `predicate.chain` "is REQUIRED on every `tool_call` and `checkpoint` record, and on every `chain_break` record whose `chainBreak.priorHead` is non-null." The fail-closed rule that enforces it, lines 748-749, names only two of the three: "A verifier MUST reject, fail-closed, a `tool_call` or `checkpoint` record that lacks `predicate.chain`." A `chain_break` with a known prior head is therefore required to carry the object, yet no verifier is obliged to reject one that does not, and the Parsing Rules bullet at lines 815-816 repeats the same two-of-three list.

The null-break case is stated two ways. Lines 734-735 say that when the prior head is unrecoverable "the break record carries no `predicate.chain`", which reads as a prohibition. Lines 812-813 say `predicate.chain` "is OPTIONAL only on `chain_break` records with `chainBreak.priorHead: null`", which permits it. A producer following the second can emit a null break carrying a `predicate.chain`, and a verifier following the first rejects it.

Proposed text for lines 748-749 and for the matching sentence at lines 815-816: "A verifier MUST reject, fail-closed, a `tool_call` or `checkpoint` record, or a `chain_break` record whose `chainBreak.priorHead` is non-null, that lacks `predicate.chain`." Proposed text for lines 812-814: "It MUST be absent on `chain_break` records with `chainBreak.priorHead: null`, which is the single case in which a chained record carries no `predicate.chain` (see Chain shape), and a verifier MUST reject, fail-closed, such a record that carries one." The corpus follows lines 734-735 today and will add vectors for both rules once the text settles them.

## 4. Replace the conformance-corpus subsection, lines 635-675

The subsection names a repository path that now redirects, directories the corpus no longer has, and a self-check script that has been replaced. Since the pinned release, the corpus has moved to `probityai/agent-evidence-vectors`, its members live together in `statements/` with the verdict in `MANIFEST.json`, and its self-check is the `aee-verify` command built from the same repository. The text below pins the release that carries this regeneration. The commit, the corpus digest and the counts are filled in from that release's `MANIFEST.json` and release notes at tag time; the specification digest is already fixed, because it is the digest of this revision's own bytes.

Proposed replacement for lines 635-675:

"The externally authored conformance corpus for this predicate is maintained at [`probityai/agent-evidence-vectors`](https://github.com/probityai/agent-evidence-vectors) under `vectors-ai-agent-action/`. The pinned release is v0.13.0, at commit `<v0.13.0 commit>`. Its `spec-vendored/` directory carries the specification text at commit `a5dd509c7476bcd7c738bee1afc3c02a57ac9e91`, and its `MANIFEST.json` publishes `corpusDigest` `<v0.13.0 corpusDigest>` and `specDigest` `5c417e9cb61fa61daaaa7ecf18a5c50f83bb4160635dc353920d3f9d3de73d4c`, which equals the SHA-256 of this specification's bytes at that commit.

An implementation claiming conformance to the text at commit `a5dd509` MUST accept every member the pinned release's `MANIFEST.json` declares with kind `accept` and MUST reject every member it declares with kind `reject`, each under the deployment profile the member names where it names one (see Chain break vs. planted state). A conformance claim is supported only by a report showing that the named verifier ran on every vector: the report's `verifier.vectorsExecuted` MUST equal the vector count of the pinned release. Every reject condition in the corpus has an accepting member carrying the same condition under the same profile, and the corpus's `aee-verify` self-check refuses a corpus in which one does not, so a verifier that rejects everything does not satisfy the corpus.

Each reject member names the text it rests on, as lines of the vendored specification with a quotation the self-check finds in them. One reject condition rests instead on a proposed rule this text does not carry, and the corpus marks it as such; an implementation conforming to this text alone accepts that member, and its report says so rather than counting it as a failure.

This subsection binds a MUST to a specific pair of bytes: the pinned release is normative for the specification text it vendors, and every subsequent revision of this document is normative-in-principle but is not certified until the next release whose `spec-vendored/` recomputes to a `specDigest` matching the revised text. Drift is detectable by recomputing `specDigest` over the vendored specification at the pinned tag and comparing it against the current text's SHA-256.

This corpus follows the external-corpus door in [in-toto/ITE#63](https://github.com/in-toto/ITE/pull/63): a conformance suite authored by someone other than the specification's author, with negative controls enforced by its self-check."

The sentence requiring `verifier.vectorsExecuted` to equal the vector count is the one addition of substance. Without it, a report that ran a verifier on a subset of the corpus, or that silently fell back to a different verifier when the named one failed to start, reads the same as a full run. The corpus harness already reports both numbers and fails a run on any gap; the specification should require a conformance claim to show them.
