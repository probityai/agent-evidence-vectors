## What this relates, and what it does not

The suite's `comparisonSurface` marks `verdict` and `result` normative and
refusal `codes` measured. So neither vocabulary is proposed here as a
correction of the other, and nothing below asks either rail to rename a
code. Both are mapped onto a third thing: the suite's own condition
identifiers, which carry line anchors into the vendored specification.

Every relation is extensional. Two codes are called `exact` when they
refuse the same statements across this corpus at this revision, which is a
fact about 209 vectors and not a claim that two definitions mean the same
thing. A pair agreeing on all of them may still part on the next one.

## Limits

- Every relation here is extensional: it says which statements two codes both refuse, across one corpus at one revision. It is not a claim that the two definitions mean the same thing, and a pair that agrees on 272 vectors may still diverge on the 273rd.
- Codes are measured and not scored under the suite's comparisonSurface, so nothing here proposes that either rail adopt the other's spelling.
- This rail refuses every reject vector, so no row below rests on a disagreement about whether a statement is invalid. The crosswalk relates reasons given for refusals both rails already share.
